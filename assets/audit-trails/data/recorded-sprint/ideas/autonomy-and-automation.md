# Autonomy and automation

Used by: [S02](../notes/S02-chiodo.md), [S03](../notes/S03-raley.md)

- **S02 Chiodo:** the central distinction. Greek *automatizo* and its relatives point to chance and spontaneity, "even the opposite of autonomy's self-determination" (p. 6). Human art results from autonomy; *Edmond de Belamy* results from "automation" (pp. 6–8). "Autonomous AI art" in her title sense means outputs "greatly autonomous from humans' inputs" (p. 2), which is a different, technical use of "autonomous."
- **S03 Raley and Rhee (appendix voices, kept separate):**
  - Bassett lists "questions concerning autonomy (which don't reduce to automation)" among the stakes of AI (p. 194).
  - Parisi says AI "menaces the autonomy of the humanities" and criticises the "Promethean promise for the autonomy, self-making, and self-determination of philosophy" (p. 197).
  - Raley and Rhee's own introduction mentions "autonomous machines" only in passing (p. 187).

**Interpretation (mine):** S02 and Bassett both refuse to collapse autonomy into automation. Parisi's critique of self-determination may sit in tension with S02's defence of it. There is no citation between them.
