# Burden and responsibility

Used by: [S01](../notes/S01-hofman.md), [S02](../notes/S02-chiodo.md), [S03](../notes/S03-raley.md)

- **S01 Hofman:** practical and professional. Designers at the "consumption stage" inherit "upstream governance and ethical considerations" (p. 4). Labels can relieve them of "part of the ethical burden" (p. 4). The framework should not be "over-burdening" the creative process (p. 1) or make ethics "an afterthought burdening creativity" (p. 6). Lightening the burden is a goal.
- **S02 Chiodo:** existential and Kantian. Autonomy is "an enormous opportunity and an enormous burden," the burden of being "individually self-determining" and responsible (p. 6). AI "may be read as what can unburden us from our autonomy's heaviest burdens" (p. 10). That wish is "understandable, and even touching" but "cannot succeed" (p. 10). Relief from the burden is the problem diagnosed.
- **S03 Raley and Rhee:** "responsibility" appears through attribution. With LLMs, determining "linguistic property and, by extension, responsibility is essentially foreclosed" (p. 190). In the appendix, Milburn (a separate voice) wants students taught to use AI "more responsibly" (p. 196).

**Interpretation (mine):** S01 and S02 use "burden" in different registers and with opposite valuations. They do not respond to each other.
