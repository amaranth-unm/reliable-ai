# Authorship and attribution

Used by: [S02](../notes/S02-chiodo.md), [S03](../notes/S03-raley.md)

- **S02 Chiodo:** proposes "the right to always know the authorship of the artefacts, both material and immaterial" (p. 10, with n. 23 on the EU AI Act). She draws on Goodman: genuineness depends on the history of production (p. 10). She extends this to not wanting "AI physicians and psychologists" (p. 11).
- **S03 Raley and Rhee:** with GPT-3, text may have "no proper subject," and attribution becomes "a pressing research question," especially for hate speech (GPT-4chan) (p. 190). Separately, they value Ridler's "direct, even authorial connection to the data, which she has collected and labeled herself" (p. 186).

**Interpretation (mine):** both treat knowing who made something as ethically significant. S02 frames it as a right of audiences; S03 frames it as a research problem and a feature of critical art practice.
