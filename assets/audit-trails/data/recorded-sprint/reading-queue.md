# Reading queue

Ordered against the brief's interest: **creative practice and how it relates to ethical concepts and historical framing** ([brief.md](brief.md)). All three judgements rest on full reads of the PDFs (see each note), so none is provisional on access. They remain my judgements, not checked by a person.

## 1. S03 · Raley and Rhee, "Critical AI: A Field in Formation" ([note](notes/S03-raley.md))

- **Next action:** read closely, then follow up on the special issue's essays.
- **Start with:** pp. 185–186 (Ridler's *Myriad (Tulips)* and "presentism"), then p. 191 (Dinkins's *N'TOO*, artists as stress-testers), then pp. 192–193 (five lines of inquiry).
- **Why it matters for the interest:** it is the only source that joins all three parts of the interest in one example. Ridler's work is creative practice, it invokes the Dutch tulip trade and the history of capital (historical framing), and it raises questions about data labour and data sovereignty (ethics). The appendix adds six other voices, including Bassett on autonomy and creation (p. 194).
- **What I read to judge this:** the whole PDF, printed pp. 185–204.
- **Caveat:** open-access status of the hosted PDF not confirmed (see [sources.md](sources.md)). Its ethics is political rather than drawn from ethical theory. Note 10 points to Stark's essay on ethics in the same issue, which was not read.

## 2. S02 · Chiodo, "What AI 'art' can teach us about art" ([note](notes/S02-chiodo.md))

- **Next action:** read the argument's core closely and test its scope line.
- **Start with:** pp. 5–7 (the Ridler quotation, Poe/Borges thought experiment, etymology of automation), then p. 10 (unburdening and the right to know authorship).
- **Why it matters for the interest:** it ties an ethical concept (autonomy as burden and responsibility) to a claim about creative practice (what makes something art), and it uses history (etymology, the classical-to-romantic shift) to do so. It is also the clearest *position* among the three, which makes it good for discussion.
- **What I read to judge this:** the whole PDF (cover sheet plus printed pp. 1–13).
- **Caveat:** the argument covers only "greatly autonomous" outputs (p. 2). It explicitly sets aside artists like Ridler who use AI as a tool, which is the kind of practice S03 centres.

## 3. S01 · Hofman, "Towards a Practical Ethics of Generative AI in Creative Production Processes" ([note](notes/S01-hofman.md))

- **Next action:** skim for the framework, then read the "try" section. Read Munn (2023) before relying on S01's use of it.
- **Start with:** pp. 9–10 ("Constraints as enablers," "Playful exploration"), then Table 1 (pp. 7–8).
- **Why it matters for the interest:** it connects ethical concepts directly to everyday creative practice and is the most practical of the three. It offers little historical framing, and its ethical theories are summarised briefly through secondary figures (pp. 5–6), so it contributes less to the historical side of the interest.
- **What I read to judge this:** the whole PDF (pp. 1–12).
- **Caveat:** a preprint with no date or venue printed on the PDF. It reports no empirical test yet (p. 4).
