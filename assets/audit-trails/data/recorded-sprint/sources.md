## Selected sources

### S01 · Hofman

- Citation (as printed on the PDF): Geert Hofman, "Towards a Practical Ethics of Generative AI in Creative Production Processes." Department Business and Media, Network Economics, University College of West-Flanders, Kortrijk, Belgium. 12 pp. The PDF itself prints no date, journal or arXiv identifier; the arXiv number comes from the URL (arXiv:2412.03579). Its latest cited access date is 7 June 2024 (PDF p. 2, n. 3).
- Article URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03579
- PDF link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.03579
- Saved file: [readings/S01-hofman.pdf](readings/S01-hofman.pdf)
- Found by: search 1
- Why it fits: proposes an ethics framework for designers using generative AI inside a creative process (the double diamond), using six ethical theories as "lenses" and calling for "playful exploration." It speaks directly to creative practice and ethical concepts.
- Read: whole PDF, pp. 1–12 (including references).

### S02 · Chiodo

- Citation (as printed on the PDF, cover sheet and p. 1): Simona Chiodo, "What AI 'art' can teach us about art," *Journal of Aesthetics & Culture* 16, no. 1 (2024): 2395511. https://doi.org/10.1080/20004214.2024.2395511. Published online 22 Aug 2024; received 11 January 2023, accepted 19 August 2024. Open access, CC BY-NC 4.0. Author affiliation: Department of Architecture and Urban Studies, Politecnico di Milano.
- Article URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/20004214.2024.2395511
- PDF link: https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/e0048d67-d8dd-48f6-abfe-e235a2411415/What%20AI%20art%20%20can%20teach%20us%20about%20art.pdf (Politecnico di Milano repository; the publisher's version with a Taylor & Francis cover sheet)
- Saved file: [readings/S02-chiodo.pdf](readings/S02-chiodo.pdf)
- Found by: search 3
- Why it fits: a philosophy-of-art argument that "autonomous" AI artefacts are not art, because art requires human "meta-sensemaking." It works through literary and art-historical examples (Poe, Borges, Picasso, Goodman) and the etymology of "automation." It brings ethical concepts (autonomy, burden, responsibility) and historical framing to creative practice.
- Read: whole PDF, 14 PDF pages (PDF p. 1 is the publisher's cover sheet; printed pp. 1–13 are PDF pp. 2–14), including notes and references.

### S03 · Raley and Rhee

- Citation (as printed on the PDF, p. 185 and pp. 193 author notes): Rita Raley and Jennifer Rhee, "Critical AI: A Field in Formation," *American Literature* 95, no. 2 (June 2023): 185–204. DOI 10.1215/00029831-10575021. © 2023 by Duke University Press. Raley: University of California, Santa Barbara; Rhee: Virginia Commonwealth University.
- Article URL: https://doi.org/10.1215/00029831-10575021
- PDF link: https://english.ucsb.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/185raley.pdf (UCSB English department site)
- Saved file: [readings/S03-raley.pdf](readings/S03-raley.pdf)
- Found by: search 4
- Access caveat: this is the publisher's typeset PDF. Its margin stamp reads "Downloaded from http://read.dukeupress.edu/… by University of California Santa Barbara user on 04 June 2023," and it is posted on a department website. I did not confirm that the article is open access under a licence. The download was public (no login), but the presenter may want to check its status before sharing.
- Why it fits: an editors' introduction that defines "critical AI" as a historically situated humanities field. It opens with artworks (Anna Ridler's *Myriad (Tulips)*/*Mosaic Virus*; Stephanie Dinkins's *Not the Only One*) as forms of critical creative practice, and it closes with an appendix of short statements by six scholars (Bassett, Berry, Fazi, Halpern, Milburn, Parisi). It speaks to creative practice, historical framing (point 1, "History and historiography") and ethics/politics.
- Read: whole PDF, 20 PDF pages = printed pp. 185–204 (introduction, appendix statements, notes, references).
