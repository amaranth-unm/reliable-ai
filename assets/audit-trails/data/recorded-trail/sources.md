## Sources

### S01
**Citation:** Ali, Syed Mustafa, Stephanie Dick, Sarah Dillon, Matthew L. Jones, Jonnie Penn, and Richard Staley. 2023. "Histories of Artificial Intelligence: A Genealogy of Power." *BJHS Themes* 8: 1–18. https://doi.org/10.1017/bjt.2023.15
**URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/C0A2B7916EDC7490B4B5A62D858D3B49/core-reader
**Access:** Open access (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). The link opens a **web page** (Cambridge Core reader), and a PDF download is offered from that page.
**Why it fits:** Historians write directly about how AI's history should be told. They reject single origin stories (Dartmouth 1956, Turing 1950) in favour of a genealogy that links AI to longer histories of management, colonialism and statecraft. It is the introduction to a themed volume, so it also maps a whole field of historical work.

### S02
**Citation:** Elam, Michele. 2022. "Signs Taken for Wonders: AI, Art & the Matter of Race." *Dædalus* (Spring 2022 issue, "AI & Society"). American Academy of Arts & Sciences. [Volume, pages and DOI not shown on the page; confirm before citing.]
**URL:** https://www.amacad.org/publication/daedalus/signs-taken-wonders-ai-art-matter-race
**Access:** Open access (CC BY-NC 4.0). The link opens a **web page** with the full text and endnotes.
**Why it fits:** A humanities scholar uses cultural history and the arts, not institutional history. Elam links current AI hype to the wonder at turn-of-the-century world's fairs, and links today's classification systems to older racial typologies and skin-colour scales. She also argues for integrating the arts and humanities into AI. It is a different historical approach from S01: interpretive and grounded in art and race studies.

### S03
**Citation:** Bory, Paolo, Simone Natale, and Dominique Trudel. 2021. "Artificial Intelligence: Reframing Thinking Machines Within the History of Media and Communication." In [edited volume title and editors to confirm], 95–. Berlin: De Gruyter. https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110740202-006
**URL:** https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/e0c31c11-cd55-4599-e053-1705fe0aef77/Bory_Natale_Trudel%20AI%202021.pdf
**Access:** Open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, stated on the first page). The link opens a **PDF** (institutional repository copy of the published version).
**Why it fits:** A third historical approach, from media and communication history. The authors challenge canonical histories of AI and recast AI as part of the history of communication. They cover cybernetics, human–computer interaction, science-fiction and media narratives, and games. The chapter includes creative and popular culture as historical evidence.
**Note:** This is a book chapter, not a journal article.
