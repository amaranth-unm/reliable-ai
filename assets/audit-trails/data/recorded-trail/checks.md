## 3. Findings

### F1. "Both criticise histories written by AI practitioners" (flattened difference / attribution)
- **Where:** synthesis.md, "The shared starting point (explicit in both)": "Both sources reject a canonical origin story centred on Dartmouth, and both criticise histories written by AI practitioners." The same premise appears in idea-map row 3, reading-queue step 2 ("compare it with S01 pp. 5–6") and notes.md S01 §6.
- **Source:** S01 p. 5, paragraph beginning "Early histories of AI were offered by reflective practitioners sharing their informed perspectives…", and n. 15. Compare S03 p. 3 [97], "Such a pantheonization testifies…" through Newell's warning.
- **What needs correcting:** S03 explicitly criticises practitioner-written histories (p. 3 [97]). S01 does not. It describes practitioner histories neutrally ("reflective practitioners sharing their informed perspectives") and lists them in n. 15 alongside critical social studies. The contrast S01 actually draws on pp. 5–6 is between the *theories of intelligence* in AI and in the social studies of science, not between kinds of history. The nearest S01 comes to criticism is "Many focused on the small (yet assertive) elite Western academic communities" (p. 5). It is ambiguous whether "many" refers to practitioner histories or to all early histories. That reading is interpretive.
- **Proposed action:** Drop "explicit in both" for this half of the sentence. Rephrase along these lines: "S03 explicitly criticises practitioner-written histories. S01 lists them alongside critical social studies, and its criticism is aimed at origin myths (p. 1) rather than at who wrote the histories." Reframe queue step 2 as a test of whether S01 shares the critique, not a comparison that assumes it does.

### F2. "Both reject a canonical origin story centred on Dartmouth" (flattened difference)
- **Where:** synthesis.md, same sentence as F1. The "Self-limiting statements" row of the comparison table also cites S03's "was not invented before the 1950s".
- **Source:** S01 p. 1: moves "beyond conventional origin myths – AI was invented at Dartmouth in the summer of 1956, or by Alan Turing in 1950". S03 p. 2 [96]: "while AI was not invented before the 1950s, it had many precursors". S03 pp. 3–4 [97–98]: the "canonical narrative" of three meetings (1955, 1956, 1958) and "founding fathers". S03 p. 6 [100]: Dartmouth is "highly regarded as a founding moment".
- **What needs correcting:** S01 rejects the idea that AI has a point of invention. S03 keeps the 1950s emergence of the field. What it criticises is the teleological, great-minds way that emergence is told, and the omission of communication. These are different targets.
- **Proposed action:** Rephrase as "Both criticise the canonical narrative, but differently. S01 rejects origin stories as such. S03 accepts that AI emerged in the 1950s and criticises the 'pantheonization' and teleology of how that emergence is told."

### F4. S01's "purpose of history": a dropped qualifier and an overstated link to refusal

Also p. 17: "As Daston suggests, to revisit these histories is to challenge what could be new." And p. 16: "another instance of the world made old, not new, by AI".
- **What needs correcting:**

  - (b) S01 *notes* refusal as a growing response in the field. It does not state refusal as the issue's own purpose. It actually says its aim is "not to critique AI for the purpose of its betterment" but to get a clearer picture. "Links this to" is interpretive.

  - (d) Row 11 overstates the difference. S01 also uses history "to challenge what could be new" (p. 17), which is close to S03's "challenges the idea that AI is unprecedented" (p. 15 [109]).
- **Proposed action:** Restore "perhaps". Rephrase to "S01 places its aim next to a 'growing politics of refusal' (p. 17)". Revise row 11 to note this shared use of history against claims of novelty, and present the refusal contrast as interpretive.

### F5. The "boundary problem": S01 is said to acknowledge it, and S03 is said to set no limits (invented relationship)
- **Where:** synthesis.md, interpretation 4: "S01 acknowledges the issue ('everywhere and nowhere,' p. 1). Neither sets explicit limits." Idea-map row 12: "S01 states the problem itself". notes.md S01 §5: "The authors admit…".
- **Source:** S01 p. 1 (last paragraph) to p. 2 (first paragraph): "'AI' is everywhere and nowhere, and so is its history.
