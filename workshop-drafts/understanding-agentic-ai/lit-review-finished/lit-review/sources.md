## Sources

### S01 (teaching)
- **Citation as printed:** Sri Yash Tadimalla, Gordon Hull, Justin Cary, Jordan Register, Daniel Maxwell, David Pugalee, and Tina Heafner, "Comprehensive AI Literacy: The Case for Centering Human Agency." The first page prints no venue or date line. It is an arXiv preprint, 2512.16656v1.
- **Publication date:** December 2025 (arXiv v1; the date comes from the arXiv identifier, not from the PDF)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16656v1
- **File:** pdfs/S01-tadimalla.pdf
- **Found by:** search 1
- **Why it fits:** A position paper on teaching AI literacy, written by a team that includes humanities faculty. It argues that AI literacy must go beyond operational skill to critical, ethical and epistemological reasoning, and says this "requires an interdisciplinary approach that draws from the humanities and social sciences." It covers both students' and teachers' agency, so it speaks to people who teach with AI and people who teach about it.

### S02 (research)
- **Citation as printed:** Lauren Klein, Meredith Martin, Andre Brock, Maria Antoniak, Melanie Walsh, Jessica Marie Johnson, Lauren Tilton, and David Mimno, "Provocations from the Humanities for Generative AI Research," arXiv:2502.19190v2 [cs.CY] 12 Jan 2026.
- **Publication date:** v1 February 2025; the downloaded v2 is dated 12 January 2026
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19190v2
- **File:** pdfs/S02-klein.pdf
- **Found by:** search 2 (it also appeared in search 5)
- **Why it fits:** Humanities scholars set out what their disciplines offer to generative AI research. The paper makes eight claims (e.g., "Models make words, but people make meaning"; "Generative AI can never be representative"), gives a working definition of humanities research and its methods, and applies them to AI. It is the strongest source here on the research side of the question.

### S03 (teaching)
- **Citation as printed:** MLA-CCCC Joint Task Force on Writing and AI, Critical AI Literacy for Reading, Writing, and Languages Workshop, *Building a Culture for Generative AI Literacy in College Language, Literature, and Writing*, October 2024. © 2024 Modern Language Association of America. The authors are listed on the title page as Kofi Adisa et al., 20 authors in all, including Matthew Kirschenbaum, Elizabeth Mathews Losh and Anna Mills.
- **Publication date:** October 2024
- **URL:** https://docs.gato.txst.edu/743895/MLA-CCCC-Joint-Task-Force-WP-3-Building-Culture-for-Gen-AI-Literacy.pdf (a copy hosted by Texas State University; the task force's index of working papers is at https://mlaai.hcommons.org/working-papers/)
- **File:** pdfs/S03-adisa.pdf (named for the first author listed)
- **Found by:** search 4
- **Why it fits:** A teaching guide from the main professional bodies for language, literature and writing. It addresses how humanities departments should build critical AI literacy for students and instructors. It is the most directly pedagogical source in the set.

### S04 (research and disciplinary theory) — from the backup set
- **Citation as printed:** Seth Perlow, "Generative Theories, Pretrained Responses: Large AI Models and the Humanities," Theories and Methodologies, *PMLA* 139.3 (2024), doi:10.1632/S0030812924000518. © 2024 The Author(s). Published by Cambridge University Press on behalf of Modern Language Association of America.
- **Publication date:** 2024 (PMLA vol. 139, no. 3; the article begins on p. 548)
- **URL:** https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/580FDCD97B52C902A6328DA9F3D5FFE6/S0030812924000518a.pdf/generative-theories-pretrained-responses-large-ai-models-and-the-humanities.pdf
- **File:** pdfs/S04-perlow.pdf (the download worked and is byte-identical to sample-pdfs/perlow-2024.pdf)
- **Why it fits:** A literary scholar argues that large AI models "render humanities discourse computable" and blur the line between the sciences and the humanities. He asks humanists to move past defensive reactions and bring interpretive theory to bear on these models. It is theory, which the brief accepts, and it speaks to researchers in the humanities.

**Limits check:** 4 sources; 2 about teaching (S01, S03); no author appears twice; arXiv supplies 2 sources (S01, S02), MLA 1 (S03) and PMLA/Cambridge 1 (S04), so no venue has more than two. All are in English and dated 2024–2026.
