# Auditing a CV

Use `alexandra-ruiz-cv-workshop.docx`, a four-page fictional CV expanded from the [Alexandra Ruiz sample](https://xanthan-web.github.io/alexandra-ruiz/cv). The added work, publications, and institutions are practice material. This is an audit exercise, not a record of a real person's qualifications.

Attach the Word file to a fresh conversation. Keep the presenter key out of that conversation. First inspect the CV yourself and note anything you would ask its author about.

## First pass

```text
Audit this CV without editing it.

Look for inconsistencies within comparable entries, missing information, possible duplicate entries, and statements that may conflict. For each finding, quote the evidence and identify the section and entry. Explain why it matters.

Separate demonstrable inconsistencies, questions for the author, and optional style preferences. Do not invent missing facts, change publication statuses, or assume that an unusual format is wrong.

Prioritize the five most useful findings, then list any others briefly. State whether you examined the document's appearance, its extracted text, or its underlying Word structure, and what you could not inspect.
```

Compare two or three findings with the actual document. A plausible diagnosis still needs evidence.

## Inspect the Word structure

```text
Now inspect the actual Word document structure, if your tools permit it. Do not edit the file.

Check for tables used to arrange ordinary text, empty or merged cells, headings implemented with direct formatting, inconsistent styles or local formatting overrides, hidden text, proofing-language differences, and hyperlink destinations.

Report only structures you can actually inspect. For each finding, identify its location, give the observed property, explain its practical effect, and suggest how I can verify it in Word. Distinguish a maintenance concern from a defect. If you only have extracted text, say which checks are unavailable; do not infer hidden structures from the layout.
```

This second pass deliberately names inspection categories. Keep its results separate from what the assistant noticed in the first pass.

## Decide on rules

```text
Propose a short style guide based on the prevailing patterns in this CV. Keep formatting decisions separate from factual questions that require my answer.

For each proposed change, identify the affected entries and explain what would change. Preserve names, titles, dates, institutions, publication statuses, and substantive wording. Do not remove repeated entries until I confirm they are duplicates.

Wait for me to approve the guide before editing. Any approved edit must be made in a new Word file, with a change list.
```

The outcome to evaluate is the quality of the evidence and the usefulness of the questions. Finding every small inconsistency is less valuable than noticing a substantive conflict and leaving the decision with the author.
