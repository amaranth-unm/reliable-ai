An assignment can give students a clear intellectual purpose and still leave a practical question unanswered. Read the same instructions as a student and as an assignment designer, then decide which observations warrant a change.
{: .lead}

## The assignment

Use Fred Gibbs's **Campus History Part 2: Archive Visit and Campus History Essay**, from Making History, HIST 1105, Fall 2026. Students visit the Center for Southwest Research, compare archival evidence with an earlier AI draft, and produce an essay for the Campus History website.

- [Original course assignment](https://fredgibbs.net/courses/making-history/campus-history-human)
- [Workshop copy of the assignment]({{ '/assets/documents/making-history-campus-history-assignment.txt' | relative_url }}) (text, September 23, 2026)
- [Part 1, which precedes it](https://fredgibbs.net/courses/making-history/campus-history-ai)
- [Student publishing guide](https://amaranth.unm.edu/campus-history/student-guide/)

The workshop copy preserves the assignment wording and turns its display boxes into ordinary headings. It includes the link to the publishing guide. An assistant should not claim to have read linked pages unless they were supplied or it actually opened them. For a classroom revision, include the schedule and other instructions students receive too.

The exercise concerns the clarity of the instructions. It does not ask AI to write the student's essay or assess a student's grade.

Treat the response as a possible reading to investigate. Ask actual students to explain the assignment back to you before concluding that the instructions are clear to them.

## Read as an assignment designer

Use the same materials in a fresh conversation. TILT's framework asks whether students understand the **purpose**, the **task**, and the **criteria for success**. The assignment need not use those exact headings to communicate them. See [TILT's resources](https://www.tilthighered.org/resources), [design checklist](https://www.tilthighered.com/assets/pdffiles/Checklist%20for%20Designing%20Transparent%20Assignments.pdf), and [explanation of using the framework flexibly](https://www.tilthighered.org/faq).

## Compare the readings

{% include prompt.html id="assignment-compare" %}

An apparent agreement between AI roles does not turn a diagnosis into a fact. Return to the instructions and ask whether the quotation supports the concern. The instructor may have explained a detail in class or put it in another document.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Prepared observations for discussion</summary>

These observations come from reviewing the assignment for this workshop. They are prepared teaching examples, not transcripts of separate AI runs.

**The student has a workable starting point.** The page describes an archive visit, what to bring, how to request boxes, and how to collect images and citation details. The finished essay has an approximately 800–1000-word target, four or five images, captions, subheadings, and a bibliography.

**The designer should recognize existing strengths.** “Skills you are practicing” explains learning purposes. The archive instructions provide a sequence of actions. “What I'm looking for” names evidence of strong work and distinguishes grade levels. A review that claims the assignment has no purpose or no criteria has missed explicit information.

**A useful question joins the two readings.** The grading section expects an “AI-Archive Comparison,” but the requirements list does not explain where to put it or whether it counts toward the essay's word target. A student could reasonably ask whether it belongs inside the public essay or in a separate reflection. The designer can trace that uncertainty to the connection between required work and grading criteria.

**The next move belongs to the instructor.** Decide where the comparison belongs and how it relates to the word target; then clarify that in the task instructions. Do not have the assistant choose a submission format or add a word count on its own. Due dates and course-wide submission arrangements may appear elsewhere, so their absence from this page is a bounded observation.
